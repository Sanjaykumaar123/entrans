DAY 2 – Embedding Theory (BOW vs GloVe vs Word2Vec vs BERT)

-------------------------------------------------------------
1) Bag of Words (BOW)
-------------------------------------------------------------
- Converts text into simple word counts.
- Example: "The phone is good" → {phone:1, good:1}
- High dimensional, sparse vector.
- Ignores meaning, context, grammar.
- Works best on small/simple datasets.
- Weak for semantic understanding.

Pros:
✔ Simple, fast, easy to implement  
✔ Works well with Naive Bayes  

Cons:
✘ No meaning of words  
✘ Cannot understand synonyms  
✘ Context-independent  


-------------------------------------------------------------
2) TF-IDF (Term Frequency – Inverse Document Frequency)
-------------------------------------------------------------
- Improvement over BOW.
- Reduces the weight of very common words (the, is, and).
- Increases weight of important, unique words.

Pros:
✔ Better than BOW  
✔ Good for classification  

Cons:
✘ Still context-blind  
✘ Still sparse vectors  


-------------------------------------------------------------
3) Word2Vec (Google)
-------------------------------------------------------------
- Predicts a word based on its neighbors.
- Words with similar meaning have similar vector values.
- Two models: CBOW and Skip-gram.
- Produces dense 300-dimensional vectors.

Example:
king - man + woman ≈ queen

Pros:
✔ Understands semantic relationships  
✔ Dense compact vectors  
✔ Good for downstream ML  

Cons:
✘ Same embedding for every context  
✘ Cannot handle word sense (bank = river bank vs money bank)  


-------------------------------------------------------------
4) GloVe (Global Vectors for Word Representation)
-------------------------------------------------------------
- Trained on global co-occurrence statistics (Wikipedia, Common Crawl).
- Similar to Word2Vec but uses matrix factorization.

Pros:
✔ Very strong pretrained embeddings  
✔ Captures global statistical meaning  

Cons:
✘ Context independent  
✘ Cannot handle polysemy  


-------------------------------------------------------------
5) BERT (Transformer-based Contextual Embedding)
-------------------------------------------------------------
- Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers.
- Uses self-attention mechanism.
- Word meaning changes based on sentence context.

Example:
“I went to the bank to deposit money.”  
“I sat on the bank of the river.”

→ “bank” has different embeddings in each sentence!

Pros:
✔ Best modern NLP method  
✔ Contextual meaning  
✔ Works for classification, QA, NER, summarization  
✔ Handles long context  

Cons:
✘ Heavy model  
✘ Requires GPU for large tasks  


-------------------------------------------------------------
6) Summary Table
-------------------------------------------------------------

METHOD      | CONTEXTUAL | TYPE        | QUALITY | USE CASES
--------------------------------------------------------------
BOW         | No         | Sparse      | Low     | Simple text ML
TF-IDF      | No         | Sparse      | Med     | Classification
Word2Vec    | No         | Dense       | Good    | NLP ML tasks
GloVe       | No         | Dense       | Good    | General embeddings
BERT        | YES        | Dense       | Best    | All NLP tasks


-------------------------------------------------------------
7) Which is Best?
-------------------------------------------------------------
- For simple datasets → BOW / TF-IDF
- For semantic understanding → Word2Vec / GloVe
- For modern NLP → BERT (Transformer-based models)
